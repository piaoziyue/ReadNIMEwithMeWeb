# Pliability as an experiential quality: Exploring the aesthetics of interaction design

## Experiential qualities**: 

**Definition:** The *subjective, felt aspects* of human-computer interaction that emerge from a person's direct engagement with a system, product, or interface. These are qualities that can only be understood through lived experience rather than objective measurement.

* Aesthetic qualities: beauty, elegance, visual appeal
* Hedonic qualities: enjoyment, fun, stimulation, novelty
* Pragmatic qualities: usability, efficiency, effectiveness
* Social qualities: connectedness, sharing, status
* Emotional qualities: trust, frustration, satisfaction, engagement

## User Experience Evaluation
* What it is: Systematic assessment of how users feel about and interact with a system through various methods (surveys, interviews, observations, physiological measures).

* How experiential qualities are involved:

* UEQ (User Experience Questionnaire) measures pragmatic vs. hedonic qualities
Likert scales capture subjective feelings (enjoyment, frustration, trust)
Think-aloud protocols reveal real-time emotional and cognitive responses
Post-task interviews elicit reflections on aesthetic appeal, satisfaction, perceived effort

* Example: Testing your breathing corset might reveal that users find it "empowering" (hedonic quality) but "initially awkward" (pragmatic quality), showing how technical function intersects with felt experience.

## Phenomenological Approaches
* What it is: Philosophy-based methods focusing on the lived, first-person experience of phenomena—understanding "what it feels like" rather than "what happens."

* How experiential qualities are involved:

* Descriptive interviews capture rich, detailed accounts of subjective experience
Interpretative phenomenological analysis identifies themes in how people make sense of interactions
Focus on embodiment and the felt sense of using technology
Temporal dimension of how experiences unfold and change

* Example: Instead of measuring breath rate changes, phenomenological research would explore "What does it feel like to have your breathing monitored?" or "How does awareness of your breath change your musical performance experience?"

## Autobiographical Design Methods
* What it is: Designers use themselves as both researcher and subject, creating deep, extended engagement with their own designs through personal use.

* How experiential qualities are involved:

* Self-as-data source provides intimate access to subjective experience
Extended temporal engagement reveals how experiential qualities evolve over weeks/months of use
Reflective documentation (journals, photos, sketches) captures nuanced feelings and insights
Design-through-experience where insights directly inform iterative improvements

* Example: You wearing your breathing corset daily during piano practice for 3 months, documenting how your relationship with the device changes—from "invasive monitoring" to "supportive awareness tool."

## Long-term Ethnographic Studies
* What it is: In-depth, naturalistic observation of people in their real contexts over extended periods (months to years).

* How experiential qualities are involved:

* Contextual richness shows how experiential qualities emerge in real-life situations
Cultural and social dimensions of how technology becomes meaningful
Longitudinal perspective reveals how experiential qualities shift as technology becomes familiar
Participant observation captures both explicit and tacit aspects of experience

* Example: Following musicians over a year as they integrate breathing sensors into their practice, observing how the technology becomes part of their identity, routine, and artistic expression.
